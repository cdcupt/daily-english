01 · Design Rationale

Warm editorial coaching, now AI-native

The product is corrective and adaptive — it should feel like a sharp, encouraging editor sitting beside you, not a cartoon classroom. The visual language is warm paper, confident serif headlines against rounded sans body, and color used as meaning: teal acts, coral corrects, violet measures growth. The existing iOS warm palette is the anchor so web and iOS are visibly the same product.

The AI-native principle: the app decides, you just practice. A learner shouldn't have to browse a library, pick a category, then pick a mode before they can begin. On open, the coach hands them the next best item and the whole loop — practice → correction → score → save → review — unfolds inline in one continuous feed. The library, the three modes, scoring, the expression bank, and spaced review are all still here; they have simply moved from being pages the user navigates to being an engine the coach drives. Fewer decisions, less chrome, more reps.

The point of view

Real situations over abstract lessons. Focused correction over error dumps. Retained expression over forgotten vocab. The whole product is one loop — practice, correct, save, review — and in v2 that loop is a single scrollable feed, not a route map.

📰 Editorial, not template

Serif display headlines, asymmetric rhythm, a feed of distinct item cards each carrying its own badge and treatment. We deliberately avoid the uniform card grid and the centered-hero-with-gradient-blob default.

Cleo, one coach with a job

A single AI coach — Cleo, our own custom cat (not an emoji) — is the Study coach voice and the celebration moment. Character, not childishness. She never carries navigation, because there is barely any navigation left to carry; the earlier multi-mascot family is deferred in favor of one consistent coach.

Mascot art: Cleo is now a raster portrait (gpt-image-2 — a friendly teal cat, warm smile, violet AI sparkle, cream backdrop), used at avatar and hero sizes and circle-cropped to the face for small avatars. The flat "The Climb" app icon is unchanged.

Type scale & pairing

Hierarchy comes from real scale contrast, not weight alone. No web font is loaded; this is a system-only stack.

Surfaces use CardStyle (18–20px radius, soft shadow) as the base raised surface; recessed surfaces use warm --paper-2. The score ring shrinks to an inline mini-ring chip in the feed and to a full hero ring on You.

Motion & interaction principles

Clarify, don't decorate

Motion shows flow: the diff tiers cascade in, the inline score chip fills, the next item slides up. No motion-for-motion's-sake.

Compositor-only

Animate transform & opacity exclusively. 240ms, cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1).

Tactile states

Hover lifts/translates; active scales to .97. Every interactive element has a designed press.

Honest loading

Coach "thinking" dots + skeletons mirror final layout so AI latency never shifts the feed (CLS < 0.1).

02 · Information Architecture

Three surfaces, not a menu

v1 carried a five-tab learner shell (Home · Scenarios · Practice · Expressions · Profile). v2 collapses that to three learner surfaces — Study (the adaptive feed where everything active happens), You (the look-back mirror), and Settings (account & preferences) — plus a transient onboarding flow and a separate operator Admin. Web and iOS share the same three-surface model: a three-item bottom tab bar on mobile, a three-item top nav on desktop.

Learner — three surfaces (web + iOS)

STUDY  (the app, really)
   One adaptive session feed. On open, the coach hands the
   user the NEXT BEST item. The whole loop happens inline:
     item → respond (voice/text) → 3-tier correction
          → inline score chip → save-to-bank → next item
   Item types (all from the old "modes" + library):
     · scenario translation    (instant correction)
     · scenario dialogue turn   (voice, light correction)
     · topic description        (record → post review)
     · resurfaced review item   (your past weak points)
   No library browse. No mode picker. No separate report.

YOU  (the mirror)
   Long-term CEFR profile + 6-dimension trends · the
   expression bank (Vocabulary · Patterns · Mistakes ·
   Saved · Scenario phrases) · review status & mastery.
   Nothing active happens here.

SETTINGS  (configure)
   Account (anonymous status · magic-link upgrade · sign
   out) · Voice & TTS preference · Native language ·
   Review reminders · Appearance · Data & privacy ·
   About + CEFR compliance copy. Server-backed — no API key.

Everything that was a verb (practice, score, save, review) lives in Study. Everything that is a noun (your profile, your bank, your history) lives in You. Everything you configure (account, voice, language, reminders, privacy) lives in Settings.

Distinct chrome (dark sidebar, dense data tables, "Operator tool" banner) signals "back office". It is not a learner surface — it lives behind role auth on a separate route segment and never appears in the learner tab bar.

Tab-bar icon set — one coherent line family

Three inline-SVG glyphs drawn in one style: 24px optical box, 1.7px rounded stroke, soft-fill-on-active. Active tab is teal (fill = --teal-soft, stroke = --teal-700); the Study spark turns coral on active. Study = a speech bubble with an AI spark (conversation + adaptive AI) — it replaces the old target/pencil glyph, which read as a quiz, not an adaptive chat-driven feed. You = a person bust with a rising growth arc + spark climbing off the shoulder (identity tied to the CEFR climb & progress) — it replaces the generic head-and-shoulders glyph, which said "account", not "your progress". Settings = sliders.

On functional icons: the emoji used elsewhere in this prototype for UI affordances (mic 🎙️, bell 🔔, globe 🌐, speaker 🔊, etc.) are mockup placeholders only. In production they become the real icon set — SF Symbols on iOS and this same inline-SVG line family (1.7px stroke, 24px optical box) on web — never shipped as emoji. The mascot is separate: Cleo is always the custom vector, not an emoji.

Study You Settings
Navigation parity: identical three-surface model on web + iOS keeps muscle memory portable. The only platform difference is placement — bottom tab bar on iOS/mobile-web, top nav on desktop-web. On web the active surface, the active You sub-tab, and the active Settings section live in the URL (shareable, back-button-correct); the Study feed itself is a stream, so "back" returns to the previous surface rather than rewinding items.

02b · Engine & Content Taxonomy

The library is now an engine

The 8 scenario categories still exist — finalized below — but learners never browse them. They are the taxonomy the coach draws from when choosing each next item. There is now a single coach, Cleo (our custom vector mascot), across every category — the earlier per-category animal family is deferred. Each category still carries its sample scenarios and goal/role/phrase structure; that metadata now rides on the feed item's context strip instead of a detail page. Express Yourself remains the home for Topic Description prompts.

The four feed item types

Everything the coach can serve. The first three are the PRD's three practice modes; the fourth is the spaced-review engine surfacing past weak points.

✍️
Scenario translationTranslate a CN prompt to natural English. Instant correction — the 3-tier diff appears the moment you submit.Vocab · patterns · phrasing
💬
Scenario dialogue turnRole-play one turn with the AI counterpart (voice-first, text fallback). Light correction — only key issues flagged inline.Interaction · fluency
🎙️
Topic descriptionSpeak 30s–2min on a prompt (Express Yourself). Post-item review — whole-performance analysis when you finish.Coherence · long-form
🔁

Why the coach picks, not the user: the adaptive next-item selector weighs recency, weak dimensions, due review items, and category coverage — the same logic that used to power Home's "today's pick" and the review scheduler, now driving the entire feed. The user keeps agency through "skip / something else" on any item, but the default is always one tap to the next rep.

🎯 Study — the adaptive session feed ★ web · iOS · the app▸

The heart of the app. A single scrollable feed. The coach serves the next best item; the user responds (voice/text); correction, score, and save all happen inline; then it rolls into the next item. No browse, no mode picker, no separate report.

Layout

  • Sticky coach strip up top: mascot avatar + one-line "what's next & why" + session progress.
  • Active item card (one of four types) with a collapsible context strip (scenario · goal · your role / AI role).
  • Inline response affordance (text box and/or voice bar).
  • On submit: inline 3-tier diff (or light flag for dialogue) → inline score chip → save pills → "Next".
  • Completed items condense above; the active item stays in focus.

Key components

  • Coach strip · feed item card · item-type badge · context strip · input box / voice bar · feedback diff · score chip · save pill · ASR retry banner · thinking dots · session summary.

Item types served in the feed

(a) Scenario-practice item (next best) (b) Translation item → instant correction (c) Dialogue turn (voice, light correction) (d) Topic-description item (record → review) (e) Review / weak-point item (resurfaced)

Inline states within an item

(f) 3-tier correction reveal (g) Inline score chip (total + CEFR) (h) Save-to-bank pill (toggles green) (i) Low-ASR retry (non-blocking, in-item) (j) Empty / first-run (coach intro) (k) Loading / coach thinking (l) Error (turn preserved, re-try / re-score) (m) Session summary / celebration

🪞 You — profile · trends · expression bank · review status ★ web · iOS · the mirror▸

The look-back surface. Nothing active happens here — it's reflect-and-review. Organized as sub-tabs so the deep bank and the long-term profile coexist without crowding.

Sub-tab: Profile

  • CEFR hero (overall + stable range, labeled AI estimate).
  • 6-dimension 30-day trend chart.
  • "Focus next" weakness chips (article usage, polite requests…).

Sub-tab: Bank

  • Five categories: Vocabulary · Patterns · Mistakes · Saved · Scenario phrases.

04 · Signature Flows

Two flows the whole product hangs on

A · One loop turn inside the Study feed

The entire learning loop is now one unbroken motion in a single feed — no navigation between practice and report. The coach serves an item; the user answers; correction, score, and save all happen inline; the next item slides up.

Coach serves itemNext-best item appears in the feed with its context strip.
RespondType or speak — inline, in the same card.
Correct (inline diff)Original → Corrected → Natural, + 1–3 focused issues, right under your answer.
Score (inline chip)Mini-ring total + CEFR estimate + disclaimer on expand.
Save (inline pill)One-tap save-candidates → You bank (pill turns green).
Next itemFeed rolls on; session summary when the user stops.

B · Mistake → resurfaced review item later in the feed

A correction isn't just shown — it becomes a scheduled item that the coach drops back into the same feed at the right time. This is the engine behind the P1 review system and the long-term ability profile, now with no separate review screen.

Mistake captured"I want check in early." flagged in a Study item.
Stored as pairoriginal → corrected → natural, tagged to scenario + weakness; lands in You bank.
ScheduledSpaced repetition sets next-review-at + mastery; You shows it as "due".
ResurfacedCoach drops a review item into the Study feed when it's due.
Re-practiced inlineSame loop turn (flow A) — answer → correct → score → save.
Mastery updatesKnown/again adjusts mastery → feeds You trends.
Why coral leads both flows: the moment of correction is the product's reason to exist. Coral marks it consistently — the struck original, the flagged turn, the retry, the resurfaced weak point — so the eye always knows "this is the thing to fix," even as it streams past in a feed.
